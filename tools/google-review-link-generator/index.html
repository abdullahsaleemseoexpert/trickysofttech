    How to get your Google review link

    A Google review link is a web address that opens the “write a review” box for your business directly, so customers do not have to search for you first. There are three ways to get one.

    1. Use this tool. Enter your Place ID in Option A and the generator builds the link in the format https://search.google.com/local/writereview?placeid=YOUR_PLACE_ID.
    2. Copy it from your Business Profile. Sign in to the Google account that manages your profile, search for your business name on Google, and choose Ask for reviews to get the share link. Paste it into Option B and the tool turns it into a QR code. See Google’s tips to get more reviews.
    3. Find your Place ID yourself. Use Google’s Place ID Finder, then add it to the link above by hand.

    Whichever way you choose, press Test link and check that your own review box opens before you print or send anything. If you do not have a verified profile yet, start with our verification guide.

    How to use this review link and QR generator

    1. Enter your business name so the printed card and messages carry it.
    2. Choose Option A or B and add your Place ID or paste your review link.
    3. Check the notes under the heading. A warning means something looks unusual, and it does not stop you.
    4. Test the link, then copy it, or download the QR code as PNG for screens and documents, or SVG for print and signs.
    5. Print the card or copy a ready-made message with your link filled in.

    If you change the colors, keep a dark code on a light background. The tool warns you when contrast is too low, because a code that will not scan is worse than a plain one.

    Where to use your review QR code

    • On the counter or reception desk. A framed A6 card is the easiest way to catch customers who are already happy at the point of sale.
    • On invoices and receipts. Add the code or short link to the footer, since customers read these after the job is done.
    • On vehicles and uniforms. A code on a van door, a technician’s clipboard, or a business card gives crews a quick way to ask on site. Use the SVG version so it stays sharp at large sizes, and test it from a distance before printing.
    • In follow-up emails and texts. Use the message templates above the same day the job finishes.
    • On your website and thank-you pages. A short link under a “thanks for your order” message is a natural place to ask.

    When and how to ask customers for reviews

    Ask soon after a good experience, while the details are fresh: right after a job is finished, a problem is solved, or a customer thanks you. Same day is best, and within a few days is fine. Make it easy by sending the direct link, and keep the request short, personal, and specific, for example mentioning the job you did.

    Ask in person first when you can, then follow up with the link. Do not send repeated reminders to people who ignored the first request. A single, polite follow-up is plenty. For a full routine, read our guide on how to get and manage customer reviews on Google, and learn how to respond to Google reviews once they arrive.

    Google review rules every business should know

    • Do not pay or reward for reviews. No discounts, free products, entries into a prize draw, or gifts in exchange for a review.
    • Do not gate reviews. Do not ask customers how happy they are first and only send the link to the happy ones. Ask everyone the same way.
    • Do not write or buy fake reviews, and do not review your own business or your competitors.
    • Do not ask staff or family to post reviews for you, and avoid posting a flood of reviews from one device or location, such as a review kiosk in your shop.
    • Reply to reviews, good and bad, and never argue with a customer in public.

    Policies change, so read the current Google Maps user contributed content policy and Google’s Business Profile review tips. Reviews are one signal in a bigger local picture, which we cover in our local reputation management guide.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is this tool free?

    Yes. It is free, needs no signup, and works entirely in your browser. The Place ID, link, and business name you type are not sent to our servers. Your last entries are saved only in your own browser so a refresh does not lose them, and the Clear button removes them.

    What is a Place ID?

    A Place ID is a unique text code that Google assigns to a place on its map, such as your business. Most start with ChIJ. You can look yours up with Google’s official Place ID Finder. It identifies your listing, so the link built from it opens the review box for your business.

    Does the link expire?

    It does not have an expiry date, and it keeps working as long as your Business Profile exists. Google does say Place IDs can change over time, for example if a listing is merged or a business moves, so test your link every few months and after any big change to your profile.

    Can customers leave a review without a Google account?

    No. A customer has to be signed in to a Google account to post a review. If a customer is not signed in, the link will ask them to sign in first, and some will give up at that point, so make the request easy and personal.

    Why is my review not showing?

    Google runs new reviews through automated checks, and a review can take anywhere from a few minutes to a few days to appear. It can also be held or removed if it looks like it breaks the content policy, for example if it seems fake, was posted from a new or flagged account, or came from a burst of similar reviews. Ask the customer to check their own contributions in Google Maps, and wait a few days before assuming it is lost.

    Can I ask for 5-star reviews?

    You can ask for a review, but do not ask for a particular rating, and do not give anything in return. Google’s guidance is to ask customers for honest feedback, not to selectively ask only the happy ones, and not to offer discounts, gifts, or other rewards. Ask everyone, accept whatever they write, and reply to every review.

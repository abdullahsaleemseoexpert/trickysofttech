    How the Local SEO audit quiz works

    The quiz asks 15 questions, three for each of five areas that influence local visibility. For each one you answer Yes, Partly, No, or Not sure. Yes earns 2 points, Partly earns 1, and No or Not sure earns 0. Your total is scaled to a score out of 100.

    • 0 to 49: Needs work. Several basics are missing, and fixing them is likely to help.
    • 50 to 74: Getting there. The foundation is in place, with gaps worth closing.
    • 75 to 100: Strong. Most basics are covered, so the focus shifts to keeping them current and beating competitors.

    This is a self-assessment based on your answers. It checks good practice, not live rankings, so treat it as a checklist that points at what to look at, not a verdict on your search position.

    The 5 areas that decide local rankings

    Google Business Profile. Your profile is what appears in the map pack and on Google Maps. It should be claimed, verified, set to the right primary category, and kept active with real photos and updates. See our Google Business Profile checklist.

    Reviews. Customers read reviews before they call, and review quantity, recency, and replies all send trust signals. Our guide to getting more Google reviews covers a simple process.

    Citations and NAP. Citations are mentions of your business name, address, and phone number (NAP) on other sites. They should match everywhere. Read about NAP consistency.

    On-page SEO. Your website should explain each service on its own page, mention the areas you serve, and use clear titles that include the service and city. See service area pages vs city pages.

    Technical SEO. Pages should load fast, work on phones, run on HTTPS, be indexable, and use LocalBusiness schema so search engines understand the business.

    For a deeper look at how these fit together, read local SEO ranking factors.

    What your score means and what to fix first

    Start with the lowest-scoring area on your results page, then work through the fixes in the order listed. As a rule of thumb, fix the foundation first: an unverified profile, a wrong primary category, or inconsistent business details can hold back everything else. Reviews and on-page improvements then compound on top.

    Your score changes as you fix things, so retake the quiz after a month or two. To check whether the work is paying off, try the Local SEO ROI calculator to model what extra leads could be worth.

    DIY fixes vs when to hire help

    Many fixes are well within reach for a business owner: claiming and verifying the profile, adding photos, replying to reviews, and correcting a phone number. These cost time, not money.

    Help tends to pay off when you serve several cities, compete in a crowded market, have a suspended or duplicate listing, or lack the time to keep up with reviews and content.     Frequently asked questions

    How often should I audit my local SEO?

    A quick check every quarter is a sensible habit, and a full review once a year. Check sooner after a website change, a move, a phone number change, or a drop in calls. This quiz takes a few minutes, so you can repeat it whenever something changes.

    What is the most important local ranking factor?

    No single factor wins on its own, and Google does not publish a ranked list. Relevance, distance, and prominence all matter. In practice, a verified and well-categorized Google Business Profile, strong recent reviews, and consistent business details are the foundation most other work builds on.

    Why am I not showing in the map pack?

    Common reasons are an unverified or suspended profile, a wrong primary category, few or old reviews, inconsistent business details across the web, or being far from the searcher. Work through the quiz results to see which of these apply to you, then read our guide on why a profile may not show up.

    How long do fixes take to work?

    It varies. Profile and website edits can be picked up within days to a few weeks. Reviews, citations, and new pages usually build over weeks to months, and competitive markets take longer. Treat the fixes as steady work, not an overnight switch.

    Is this audit really free?

    Yes. The quiz and your score are free and are never hidden behind a form. You can optionally ask for a free expert review of your gaps afterward, which is also free and carries no obligation.

    What happens after I submit my details?

    Your name, email, website, and quiz answers are sent to our team. We review the gaps and contact you by email, and by phone only if you gave a number. Your details are used for that follow-up, as described in our privacy policy.

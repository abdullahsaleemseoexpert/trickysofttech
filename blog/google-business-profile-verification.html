Google Business Profile Verification: Every Method, How Long It Takes, and What to Do When It Fails

Verification is the step between a profile that exists and a profile customers can actually find. Here is every method Google offers, how to pass video verification, and how to recover when it fails.

Quick answer: To verify a Google Business Profile, sign in to the Google account that manages it, open the profile, choose Get verified, and complete whichever method Google offers you: a text message, phone call, email, mailed postcard, recorded video, or a live video call. Which options appear depends on your business and your account history, and you cannot choose a method Google has not offered. Instant methods finish in minutes, a postcard takes days, and a video is reviewed by Google, often within a few business days.

A Google Business Profile that is not verified is close to invisible. It does not appear in Google Search or Google Maps, it cannot collect public reviews, and none of the work you put into photos, services, and descriptions can help you rank. Verification is the step that proves the business is real, that it operates where it says it does, and that you are allowed to manage it. For a local or home service business, it is also the step where most launches stall.

This guide covers every verification method Google currently offers, how long each one tends to take, how to prepare for video verification (now the most common route for service businesses), the reasons verification fails, and what to do when a postcard never arrives or a request is rejected. Google changes the flow regularly, so treat the screens in your own account as the final word and use this guide to prepare for them.

What verification actually proves

Google verifies three things before it lets a profile go public:

  • The business exists. There is a real operation behind the name, not a listing created to capture leads or occupy a spot on the map.
  • It is where it says it is. The address you entered is a place where the business actually operates or, for a service area business, is based.
  • You have authority over it. The person requesting control is the owner or an authorized manager, not a competitor or a stranger.

That is why Google is stricter than most owners expect. Map spam is a large and persistent problem, especially in trades where a single phone call is worth hundreds of dollars. If you want the other side of this, our guide to reporting fake Google Business listings and map spam explains what Google is trying to filter out, and why legitimate businesses sometimes get caught in the same net.

Verification is not a ranking factor in itself, and a verified profile is not guaranteed to rank. It is the entry ticket. Everything that decides your position afterward, relevance, distance, and prominence, is covered in how to rank in the Google map pack and local SEO ranking factors in 2026.

The practical takeaway: if Google offers an instant method, a text, a call, or an email, take it. Those are the least painful and carry the lowest risk of a rejected attempt. If you are given only a postcard or a video, that is the route Google has decided on for your profile, and the best use of your time is to prepare well for it rather than to retry different options.

How to verify, step by step

  1. Make sure you are signed in to the right Google account. Use the account that created or manages the profile, ideally an account the business itself controls, not an employee's personal login or an agency's account you may lose later.
  2. Find your profile. Search for your business name while signed in, or open Business Profile management from your account. You should see a prompt such as "Get verified" or "Verify now."
  3. Check the details Google is about to verify. Confirm the business name, address, phone number, category, and service area are final and accurate. Changing them mid-process can invalidate a postcard code or trigger a fresh review.
  4. Choose the method Google offers. Select it and read the instructions on screen before you proceed. For a postcard, confirm the mailing address, including any suite or unit number. For a video, read the checklist first.
  5. Complete the method. Enter the code, click the email link, or record and upload the video, depending on the route.
  6. Wait for confirmation. The status in your profile changes to "Verified" or you receive a notice. Do not resubmit while a request is marked pending unless Google asks you to.
  7. Complete the profile and check it publicly. Once verified, search for your business in a private browser window and confirm it appears. Then work through the Google Business Profile optimization checklist.

Eight things to fix before you request verification

Most failed verifications trace back to something that could have been fixed in advance. Check these before you click the button.

  • Use your real business name. The name on the profile should match what appears on your signage, invoices, and website. Adding keywords or a city to the name is a common reason for rejection and suspension.
  • Use a real, staffed address. A PO box, mailbox rental store, or virtual office is not eligible. A home address is acceptable for a service area business if you hide it from public view.
  • Choose the right primary category. A category that does not match what you do invites questions. See how to choose your Google Business Profile categories.
  • Match your phone number everywhere. The number on the profile should match your website and listings, so Google sees one consistent business. Inconsistent details are covered in NAP consistency and local rankings.
  • Have a working website. A site that clearly names the business, the services, and the area reassures reviewers and may unlock email or instant verification.
  • Set accurate hours. Hours that say 24/7 for a one-person shop raise doubts. Be honest about availability.
  • Make sure the mailbox is monitored. A postcard sent to an address no one checks is a common cause of "it never arrived."
  • Confirm you own the account. Know who controls the Google account and recovery details, so you are not locked out mid-process.

Passing video verification the first time

Video verification has become the standard for many service businesses, and it is where owners most often get stuck, usually because nobody told them what the reviewer needs to see. Google's prompts vary, but the goal is always the same: prove, in one unbroken recording, that a real business operates at this location and that you run it.

What to record

Plan a continuous walk-through in this order, and keep the camera moving smoothly rather than cutting between clips:

  1. The surrounding area. Start outside, showing the street, nearby landmarks, and the entrance so the reviewer can tie the footage to the address.
  2. Signage and business identity. Show your business name on a sign, a marked vehicle, a uniform, or a door. For a home-based service business, a branded vehicle parked at the address is often the clearest evidence.
  3. The place of work. Show the shop, garage, office, or storage area where the business operates.
  4. Tools, equipment, and inventory. Show the equipment you use for the work, such as tools, parts, supplies, or machinery, and your vehicles if you travel to customers.
  5. Proof that you manage the business. Be ready to show a business license, an invoice or work order with your business name, or similar paperwork that connects you to the operation.

What to show by type of business

Business typeStrongest evidence to film
Plumber, HVAC, electricianBranded van with ladder racks and tools, parts shelves, the place where the van is based, a work order showing the business name
LocksmithMarked vehicle, key-cutting and lock equipment, licensing paperwork where required in your state, the base location
TowingTrucks with the business name, the lot or yard, dispatch equipment
Junk removal or movingBranded trucks, equipment, the storage or staging area
Cleaning or landscapingUniforms, branded vehicles, supplies and equipment, the storage location
StorefrontExterior signage from the street, the interior sales or service area, opening hours posted on the door

What to avoid

  • Cutting or editing the footage. Google wants one continuous take. Stitched clips look staged.
  • Filming only a document. A license on a table proves nothing about the location. Show the place first, then the paperwork.
  • Using stock imagery, screenshots, or footage of someone else's building. This is a fast way to a rejection.
  • Filming at a different address than the one on the profile. The footage has to match the address Google has on record.
  • Rushing. A steady, clear, well-lit recording is easier to review than a blur of quick pans.

If you do all of this and the request is still rejected, the cause is usually a mismatch somewhere else, such as the name, address, or category. The next section covers how to diagnose it.

Two points are worth repeating. First, retrying the same failed attempt without changing anything rarely works and can slow you down. Fix the underlying cause first. Second, a rejected verification is not the same as a suspension, but the two can overlap, so read the exact message Google gives you before deciding what to do next.

When the postcard never arrives

A postcard that takes a week is normal. One that takes much longer deserves a calm checklist rather than a flurry of requests.

  1. Wait the full window. Postal delivery varies, and Google's own guidance allows up to about two weeks.
  2. Check the address on the profile. A missing suite or unit number is the most common cause of a lost card.
  3. Check who collects the mail. Ask the building, the landlord, or anyone else who might have thrown away what looked like junk mail.
  4. Request a new code once. Use the option in your profile only after the wait has passed, and use only the newest code.
  5. If a second card fails, switch routes. If another method becomes available, such as a call or a video, take it. If not, contact Google Business Profile support through the help flow in your account.

Special cases that trip people up

You moved, or the business has two addresses

Changing the address on a verified profile can trigger re-verification. Update the address only when the move is real, and be ready to verify again at the new location. If you want to keep a service area while changing where you are based, review the service area business guide first.

You are taking over an existing listing

Many profiles already exist for a business before the owner claims them. If someone else is listed as owner, you can request ownership from the profile, and the current owner is given a window to respond. If they do not respond, you can usually proceed to verification. Keep your evidence, such as licenses and invoices, ready in case Google asks.

A business with more than one location

Each location needs its own profile and its own verification. Businesses with many locations can use bulk verification, which Google reserves for larger chains. If you run several locations, multi-location local SEO explains how to keep names, addresses, and categories consistent so verification goes smoothly.

A brand-new business with no signage yet

Get the basics in place before you apply: a branded vehicle or sign, a business license, an invoice template, and a simple website. A new business with no visible footprint is the one most likely to face repeated requests.

A home-based business that does not want the address public

Use the real address for verification and hide it from the public profile by marking the business as a service area business. Do not enter a fake address or a PO box. Our service area guide explains the exact setting.

A coworking space, virtual office, or shared address

These are frequent sources of rejection because Google wants a place where the business actually operates with staff present. A mail-forwarding address or a virtual office is not eligible. A genuinely staffed shared workspace can be acceptable, but only if customers can reach you there during your stated hours.

Your first week after you verify

Verification unlocks the profile, but a verified empty profile does very little. Use the first week to do the work that actually earns visibility:

  • Choose and confirm your categories. Set a primary category and a few accurate secondary ones.
  • List your services. Add every service you actually offer, in plain language. See how to write Google Business Profile services.
  • Set your service area honestly. Include the places you genuinely serve, not an oversized radius.
  • Add real photos. Include your team, vehicles, tools, and finished work.
  • Start collecting reviews. Ask every customer after a completed job. The process is in how to get Google reviews, and how to respond to them matters just as much.
  • Check consistency across the web. Align your listings using local citations and fix mismatches with citation cleanup.
  • Add structured data to your site. A LocalBusiness schema block reinforces the same name, address, and phone number on your website.
  • Baseline your rankings. Record where you appear now, using the methods in Google Maps rank tracking, so you can see the effect of later work.

Why Google asks you to re-verify, and how to avoid it

A profile that was verified years ago can suddenly ask to be verified again. It does not always mean something is wrong, but it is worth treating as a signal. Common triggers include:

  • Edits to the name, address, or primary category. These sensitive fields are the most likely to reset verification.
  • An ownership change or new manager added. A new controller is a reason for Google to recheck.
  • Unusual activity on the profile. Many edits in a short period, or edits that revert, look like instability.
  • Spam-pattern signals. Keyword-stuffed names, shared addresses, or several profiles at one location draw attention.
  • Random review cycles. Google sometimes revisits older profiles as part of broader quality checks.

The way to avoid it is boring and effective: make fewer changes to sensitive fields, make them one at a time, keep the account secure and controlled by the business, and keep your details consistent everywhere. When you do need to change a sensitive field, do it deliberately rather than as part of a general clean-up.

Ten mistakes that delay verification

  1. Adding keywords or a city to the business name. It looks like spam and it often is treated as spam.
  2. Using a PO box, virtual office, or forwarding address. None of them is eligible.
  3. Requesting several postcards in a row. Only the latest code works, and repeated requests cause confusion.
  4. Editing the profile while a request is pending. Changes can invalidate a code or restart a review.
  5. Filming a rushed or edited video. Continuous, steady footage is the standard.
  6. Verifying under a personal account you may later lose. If the account is closed, you may lose control of the profile.
  7. Choosing a category that does not match the work. Mismatches create questions.
  8. Letting someone else handle it without access. If an agency verifies under its own account, make sure you are added as an owner.
  9. Creating a duplicate profile after a rejection. Duplicates make matters worse and can lead to suspension.
  10. Giving up too early. Many verifications that look stuck resolve after the cause is corrected and one clean attempt is made.

Verification, rankings, and AI answers

People increasingly ask AI assistants and Google's AI features for local recommendations, and those systems lean on the same trusted signals that verified, complete profiles carry: a consistent name, address, and phone number, genuine reviews, accurate categories, and a website that matches. A profile that never completes verification contributes nothing to any of this. A verified one, kept consistent and active, gives AI systems something reliable to cite.

Common Questions

Frequently asked.

How do I verify my Google Business Profile?
Sign in to the Google account that manages the profile, open it, and choose Get verified. Then complete the method Google offers: a text message, phone call, email, mailed postcard, recorded video, or a live video call. You cannot pick a method Google has not offered, and the options depend on your business and account history.
How long does Google Business Profile verification take?
Instant, text, call, and email methods usually finish in minutes. A postcard commonly takes several days and can take up to about two weeks. Video verification is reviewed by Google, often within a few business days, though review times vary.
Why am I only offered video verification?
Google chooses the method based on how risky it judges the profile to be. Service area businesses with hidden addresses, home-based businesses, new profiles, and spam-prone categories are more often asked for video. It is not a punishment, and the best response is to prepare the video carefully.
What do I need to film for video verification?
One continuous recording that shows the surrounding area and entrance, your business name on a sign or vehicle, the place where you work, your tools and equipment, and proof that you manage the business, such as a license or work order. Do not cut or edit the footage, and film at the address on the profile.
Can I verify a Google Business Profile without a postcard?
Yes, if Google offers another method for your profile, such as a text, call, email, instant verification through Search Console, or video. If a postcard is the only option shown, you will need to wait for it or wait for another method to become available.
Why was my Google Business Profile verification rejected or failed?
The most common causes are an incomplete address, a name with added keywords, a PO box or virtual office, a video with cuts or no visible signage and equipment, or details that changed after the request. Fix the underlying cause before trying again, because repeating the same attempt rarely works.
Do I have to verify a service area business with a hidden address?
Yes. Google still needs a real address for verification, usually a home or office, even though you can hide it from the public profile. A PO box or virtual office is not eligible.
Can my Google Business Profile lose verification?
Yes. Google can ask you to re-verify after edits to sensitive fields such as the name, address, or category, after an ownership change, or when it sees unusual activity. Making fewer sensitive changes and keeping your details consistent reduces the chance.

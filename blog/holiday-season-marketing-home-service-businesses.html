Holiday Season Marketing for Home Service Businesses: A Week-by-Week Plan for November to January

Cold snaps, hosting, travel, and closed competitors push urgent searches toward whoever shows as open and answers the phone. Here is how to prepare, week by week, and turn winter jobs into recurring customers.

Quick answer: The holiday season is one of the best stretches of the year for home service businesses that stay visible and answerable, because cold snaps, hosting, travel, and closed competitors push urgent searches toward whoever shows as open. The plan is simple: in mid-November, fix your Google Business Profile hours and holiday dates, make sure a real person answers every call you advertise, and put seasonal services and photos on your profile and website. Through December, post short updates, request reviews after every job, and keep a storm or emergency routine ready. In January, convert holiday customers into maintenance plans and reviews that carry you into spring.

Most owners treat November through early January as either a rush to survive or a quiet stretch to coast through. Both approaches leave money on the table. Customers search differently in these weeks. They are home more, hosting more, and travelling more. Furnaces and water heaters work harder, pipes freeze, ovens are asked to do more than usual, and a lot of businesses run reduced hours or close entirely. The searches that result are urgent, local, and often made on a phone in the evening.

This is a practical, week-by-week plan for local and home service businesses: what customers need by trade, what to set up on Google, how to handle calls honestly, what to put on your website, how to use offers without giving away margin, and how to turn winter jobs into recurring customers by spring. Everything here can be done without a redesign or a large budget, and none of it depends on invented numbers. Use the parts that fit your trade and your capacity.

Why the holidays change how customers search

Four things shift at once, and each one favours the business that prepared:

  • Demand moves toward emergencies. A no-heat call, a burst pipe, or a dead refrigerator on a holiday week cannot wait. Searchers use phrases like "emergency," "open now," and "near me," and they call the first credible result.
  • Fewer competitors are open. Many companies close for the holiday itself or run a skeleton crew. If your profile accurately shows you as open, you are visible in open-now searches that others have dropped out of.
  • Hosting and travel create planned work. Pre-guest cleaning, appliance fixes before a big meal, holiday lighting, chimney inspections, and post-holiday junk removal are all booked in advance. The business that is easy to book wins them.
  • Weather drives timing. A cold front or a storm turns a quiet week into a busy one overnight. Businesses that have a ready routine capture the spike. Everyone else is catching up.

The catch is that every one of these opportunities depends on trust in the first ten seconds: your hours are right, your phone is answered, your profile looks active, and your reviews are recent. Set up your Google Business Profile for the season

Your profile is where most urgent holiday searches are decided. These are the items that matter, in order of impact.

  1. Hours and holiday dates. Make sure regular hours are correct, then add special hours for Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve, Christmas Day, New Year's Eve, and New Year's Day. Confirm each date even when your hours do not change. Our full guide to Google Business Profile hours walks through the steps, the limits, and the emergency and on-call options for service businesses.
  2. Services and description. Add the winter services you actually offer, such as frozen pipe repair, emergency heating, or snow removal, and make sure the description says where you work and how customers reach you. Keep it factual. Do not add services you do not perform.
  3. Photos. Post recent photos of real jobs, your crew, and your vehicles. Seasonal images, such as lights installed, cleared driveways, or a repaired furnace, tell a searcher you are active right now. See before and after photos for local leads.
  4. Posts. Share short updates about availability, holiday schedules, and seasonal tips. Keep the text clear, and use the profile's call and website buttons for contact details. Check Google's current posting rules before you publish, since they change, and avoid putting phone numbers in the post text.
  5. Reviews. Recent reviews carry weight at the moment of decision. Ask every satisfied customer, and reply to every review. The next section covers how.
  6. Check the public view. Search for your business in a private browser window and confirm the hours, the services, and the call button all work.

If you need a full pass over the profile, use our Google Business Profile optimization checklist. If you want help managing it through the season, that is part of our Google Business Profile management service.

Make sure the phone gets answered

The best profile in the world loses to an unanswered call. The holiday season stretches small teams, because the same person who is quoting jobs is also covering the phone and the schedule. Decide in advance how every call will be handled.

SituationWhat to set up
You are open and staffedA person or answering service who can quote and book, and a rule for how fast missed calls get a response
You are closed for the holiday itselfSpecial hours showing closed, a clear voicemail and website note, and a plan for the first business morning
You offer emergency serviceA real on-call number, a defined dispatch process, and only the hours you can truly cover
You are overbookedAn honest wait time, a waitlist, or a referral. A truthful "next Tuesday" beats a missed promise
You miss a call anywayAn automatic text back within minutes, so the customer is not lost to the next search result

A customer who calls three businesses will usually book the first one that picks up or replies. For setup options, read our guide to call handling systems for local service businesses. And do not claim 24/7 hours unless a person answers and a crew can roll at 2 a.m. The hours guide above explains why false 24-hour hours backfire.

Update your website for the season

Customers who find you on Google often check your website before they call. Make sure it agrees with your profile.

  • Holiday banner. A short, accurate notice about holiday hours and emergency availability, placed where it is seen on every page.
  • A visible phone number and booking path. One tap to call on mobile, and a form or booking link for non-urgent work.
  • Seasonal service content. A short section or page for the winter services you offer, with the area you serve and what a customer should expect.
  • Footer and contact page hours. They must match the Google profile exactly, including the holiday exceptions.
  • Opening hours markup. If your site uses LocalBusiness markup, update it so structured data and profile agree. See our guide to LocalBusiness schema.
  • Fast loading on phones. Most urgent searches are on a phone, often on weak signal. Remove anything heavy from the home page for the season.

The aim is to convert the visit into a call or a booking. Our guide on turning website visitors into booked jobs covers what a page needs to do that.

Publish the pages customers search for in a crisis

Urgent holiday searches are often questions before they are purchases: what to do when a pipe freezes, why a furnace keeps shutting off, how to stop a lockout from happening again. A short, honest page that answers one of these questions can put your business in front of someone at the exact moment they need help, and it gives you something useful to link from your profile posts.

  • Write for the emergency, not the algorithm. Lead with the immediate safe step, such as shutting off the water, then explain when to call a professional and what you will do when you arrive.
  • Tie each page to a service and an area. A guide on frozen pipes should link to your plumbing service and to the cities you actually cover, so a reader can book without hunting. Our locations page shows how we organize service areas.
  • Keep it short and current. A page of a few hundred well-organized words, updated each winter, beats a long article nobody finishes at 10 p.m. in a cold house.
  • Add your real contact path. A tap-to-call button and your honest hours belong on every one of these pages.
  • Include a photo or two from real jobs. It signals that a real crew stands behind the advice.

Publishing two or three of these before the first hard freeze is enough. They keep working after the holidays, because winter returns every year and the page only needs a light refresh. If you serve more than one city, make sure each service area page says plainly which services and hours apply there, as covered in service area pages versus city pages.

Offers without discounting yourself

The holidays tempt owners to slash prices. For most trades that is unnecessary, because urgent customers are buying availability and trust, not the lowest number. Consider offers that protect your margin:

  • Priority scheduling for plan customers. Maintenance plans for HVAC, plumbing, and chimney service give recurring customers first call during busy weeks.
  • Gift certificates. Cleaning, detailing, and lawn care can sell prepaid services as gifts, which brings in new customers through existing ones.
  • Bundled seasonal checks. A winterization or pre-guest package priced clearly, instead of an open-ended discount.
  • Referral thank-yous. A modest credit for referrals that fits your margin and does not create awkward obligations.
  • Transparent pricing language. Customers hate surprises more than they hate high prices. Explain how you quote, and what changes the price. Our junk removal marketing guide shows how price transparency builds trust in a category with little brand loyalty.

Whatever you offer, only advertise what you can deliver in the weeks that follow. A promotion that creates more bookings than you can handle damages the reviews you are trying to earn.

Reviews: ask early, answer everything

Seasonal customers decide quickly, and recent reviews are what they scan. Build review requests into the end of every job during these weeks.

  • Ask at the moment of relief. A homeowner whose heat is back on is happier than they will be next week. Send the review link by text before you leave the driveway.
  • Make it one tap. A direct review link beats instructions to search for your business.
  • Reply to every review. Thank positive reviewers by name, and answer negative ones calmly, with a way to resolve the issue offline. Replies are visible to everyone who reads.
  • Mention the service naturally. You cannot script reviews, but you can ask customers to describe what you fixed, which helps future searchers.

For the full approach, read how to get Google reviews, how to respond to Google reviews, and local reputation management.

Should you spend on ads during the holidays?

For emergency trades, paid placements such as Local Services Ads can capture urgent searches that organic results miss, but they only pay off if you can answer the leads. Before you raise a budget, confirm three things: your phone is staffed during the hours your ads run, your crew can accept the extra jobs, and you can measure which leads become paid work. If you cannot answer the first, pause the spend rather than paying for missed calls. Our guide to Google Local Services Ads for home services explains how they work and what to watch, and how much to spend on marketing helps you size a seasonal budget.

Build a storm and cold-snap routine

Weather turns a normal week into a spike with little warning. A short routine, written down before it happens, lets you respond in an hour instead of a day.

  1. Watch the forecast. Assign someone to check it daily and flag a hard freeze or storm 48 hours ahead.
  2. Update public information. Adjust special hours if needed, change the website banner, and post a short availability note.
  3. Confirm capacity. Check crew, vehicles, parts, and fuel, and decide how many extra jobs you can truthfully accept.
  4. Route the phones. Make sure the on-call line and text-back are working before the first call comes in.
  5. Reset afterward. Restore normal hours and remove temporary notices, and ask for reviews from storm-week customers.

After the holidays: turn customers into 2027 revenue

The weeks after New Year are when seasonal work becomes steady work, if you follow up. Use the first two weeks of January for four things:

  • Offer maintenance plans. A customer you just helped trusts you most right now. Invite them to a plan before the memory fades.
  • Harvest outstanding reviews. Send a polite reminder to customers who have not yet reviewed.
  • Publish what you learned. Share a short post or page about the winter jobs you completed, with photos and tips.
  • Plan spring. Pre-book recurring work and open your spring calendar early.

Then measure what happened. Count calls by source, booked jobs, review growth, and missed calls, and compare them against last year if you have the data. Tracking local SEO ROI explains how to do this without complicated tools.

Ten holiday mistakes that cost jobs

  1. Not setting holiday hours. A profile that says hours might differ sends urgent customers elsewhere.
  2. Claiming 24/7 without 24/7 staffing. Missed calls lead to bad reviews.
  3. Letting calls go to voicemail with no follow-up. A text back within minutes recovers many of them.
  4. Advertising capacity you do not have. Overpromising during a rush damages your reviews.
  5. Ignoring the website banner. Customers who check your site should see the same hours as Google.
  6. Waiting to ask for reviews. The best moment is the end of the job, not a week later.
  7. Discounting by default. Urgent customers care more about availability and trust than a few dollars.
  8. Posting once and disappearing. A profile that has been quiet for months looks abandoned.
  9. Forgetting to restore normal hours. Leaving special hours or a closed notice on after you return silently loses jobs.
  10. Skipping the January follow-up. The relationship you built during the rush is worth most if you act on it.

Holiday searches on phones, voice, and AI tools

Many holiday searches are spoken: someone asks a phone or a car assistant for a plumber who is open now. Answers like these rely on consistent facts about your business, especially your hours, your category, and your service area. When your profile, website, and listings agree, a system can answer confidently. When they conflict, it may skip you. That is why the hours and the website changes above matter beyond ordinary search. See our guides to AI and voice search optimization and how local businesses show up in AI answers for more.

Your holiday readiness checklist

  • Regular hours are correct and special hours are set for every holiday date that matters to you.
  • Every advertised phone number is answered, or has an automatic text back and a clear voicemail.
  • The profile shows winter services, recent photos, and an accurate description.
  • The website banner, footer, and contact page match the profile.
  • A review request goes out after every completed job.
  • Your storm routine is written down and someone owns it.
  • Offers protect margin and match your real capacity.
  • Ad spend, if any, matches the hours you can answer.
  • January follow-up is scheduled: plans, reviews, and a spring calendar.
  • Results are tracked so next year starts from data, not memory.

Common Questions

Frequently asked.

When should home service businesses start holiday marketing?
Mid-November is a good starting point, because hours, phone routing, and profile details should be right before Thanksgiving week. Setting special hours two to three weeks before each holiday date leaves time for Google to review the change.
Which home service trades get busiest around the holidays?
Heating, plumbing, and electrical calls tend to rise with cold weather and heavy use at home. Chimney, snow removal, and water damage services follow the weather, while cleaning, appliance repair, holiday lighting, and junk removal follow hosting and decorating. Demand varies by climate and market.
Should I change my business hours for Thanksgiving and Christmas?
Set special hours for each holiday date on your Google profile, marking the day closed or entering reduced hours. Confirm dates even when your hours stay the same. Then make your website, other listings, and phone greeting match.
Do I need to offer discounts to win holiday customers?
Usually not. Urgent customers choose on availability, trust, and recent reviews. Priority scheduling for plan customers, gift certificates, and clearly priced seasonal packages protect your margin better than broad discounts.
How should I handle emergency calls on Christmas Day?
Decide in advance who answers, who is on call, and what you can truthfully promise. Post only the hours you can cover, set an automatic text back for missed calls, and explain your emergency process on your website.
How do I get more reviews during the holiday rush?
Ask at the end of every completed job, when the customer is most relieved, and send a direct review link by text. Reply to every review, and send a polite reminder in early January to customers who have not yet reviewed.
Should I run paid ads over the holidays?
Only if your phone is staffed during the hours the ads run and your crew can accept the extra jobs. If calls would go unanswered, pause the spend instead of paying for missed leads.
What should I post on my Google Business Profile during the holidays?
Short updates about availability and holiday schedules, recent photos of real jobs, and seasonal tips tied to the services you offer. Use the profile buttons for contact details, and check Google current posting rules before you publish.

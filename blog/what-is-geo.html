What Is Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) and Why It Matters

AI Overviews, ChatGPT, and Perplexity are changing how people find a local business before they ever click a link. Here is what GEO actually is and where it fits next to SEO.

A growing share of searches never produce a list of ten blue links anymore. Someone asks Google directly and gets an AI Overview summarizing an answer at the top of the page. Someone asks ChatGPT or Perplexity a question and gets a synthesized response with a few cited sources, not a search results page at all. Generative Engine Optimization, GEO, is the practice of making sure a business is understood, represented, and ideally cited correctly inside that new layer of search.

A working definition

GEO is the set of practices that help AI systems, generative search features, and chat-based assistants correctly understand what a business does, where it operates, and why it's a credible answer to a relevant question, and, where possible, cite or recommend that business directly. It sits next to SEO rather than replacing it: SEO is largely about being found and ranked in a list of results, GEO is about being understood and referenced inside a generated answer.

How GEO relates to SEO

The two disciplines share a lot of the same foundation. A business with strong SEO, clean structured data, clear service descriptions, a well-optimized Google Business Profile, and credible reviews, is already most of the way toward being legible to AI systems too, since many of these systems draw on the same underlying web content and, for local queries, the same Business Profile data. GEO adds a layer on top: writing content in a way that answers questions directly and completely, making sure structured data explicitly states facts an AI system might otherwise have to infer, and paying attention to how a business is described across the web as a coherent, consistent entity, not just optimizing individual keywords.

This overview deliberately stops short of a full side-by-side comparison. A dedicated breakdown of where SEO and GEO diverge, and which to prioritize when, covers that ground properly elsewhere in this series.

Why this matters now

The shift is showing up directly in how people research before calling a local business. Someone comparing HVAC companies might ask an AI assistant "which HVAC company near me has the best reviews for AC repair" and get a synthesized answer rather than a list to click through and compare manually. If a business isn't clearly and consistently represented across the sources that answer draws from, it simply doesn't get mentioned, regardless of how good the actual service is.

What AI engines are doing differently

Traditional search engines crawl and index web pages, then rank them for a given query. Generative and AI-powered search tools work differently: they retrieve relevant information from indexed content or live web sources, then synthesize a direct answer, often citing a small number of sources rather than displaying a full ranked list. Google's AI Overviews draw on Google's own search index and local data. Chat-based tools like ChatGPT and Perplexity, when browsing is enabled, retrieve and cite from live web sources in response to a specific query. The mechanics differ between platforms, but the shared thread is synthesis over listing, and a much smaller number of sources actually get referenced compared to a traditional ten-result page.

Entity clarity: the core mechanic

Underneath the platform-specific differences, most of GEO reduces to a single underlying idea: helping AI systems build a confident, unambiguous picture of a business as a single entity, rather than a scattered collection of facts they have to piece together or guess at. A search engine or AI system that finds the same business name, service list, and location described consistently across your website, your Business Profile, review platforms, and any press or directory mentions can build that confident picture quickly. One that finds conflicting or incomplete versions of the same business has to either guess, which produces errors, or simply leave the business out of a generated answer rather than risk citing something uncertain.

This is why a lot of GEO work looks, on the surface, like ordinary good business hygiene: consistent naming, complete service descriptions, accurate structured data, credible third-party corroboration. The AI-specific value isn't a separate set of tricks, it's that these systems are less forgiving of ambiguity than a human reader who can use context and common sense to fill in gaps.

What GEO does not mean

  • It does not mean abandoning SEO. Foundational SEO work still drives the traditional search traffic that remains the majority of most businesses' organic visibility, and it feeds directly into GEO's own foundation.
  • It does not guarantee citation. No legitimate practice can promise a business will be specifically named in an AI-generated answer, and any service claiming otherwise is overselling.
  • It is not a trick or a loophole. The practices that help, clarity, accuracy, structured data, credible third-party signals, are the same practices that make a business genuinely easier to understand, for AI systems and humans alike.

A real example

Guardian Locksmith Co. had strong traditional local rankings, but as more locked-out customers started asking Siri, Google Assistant, and AI search tools directly instead of typing a search, they were invisible in every one of those answers. Competitors with objectively worse reviews were getting recommended instead, simply because their information was structured in a way AI tools could actually read and use. Strong traditional rankings and strong AI-search visibility are related but not identical, and a business can genuinely lead in one while being invisible in the other.

Common Questions

Frequently asked.

Is GEO a replacement for SEO?
No. GEO builds on the same foundation as SEO and adds practices specific to how AI and generative search tools retrieve and synthesize information. Most businesses need both.
Can any agency guarantee my business will be cited by ChatGPT?
No legitimate provider can guarantee a specific citation in an AI-generated answer, since these systems make that decision dynamically per query. What can be improved is the underlying clarity and credibility of your information, which improves the odds.
Do AI Overviews and ChatGPT pull from the same information?
Not identically. Google AI Overviews draw primarily from Google's own search index and local business data. Chat-based tools with browsing draw from live web sources they retrieve in response to the query. Both benefit from a business having clear, consistent, well-structured information available online.
Does my business need a big content overhaul to start on GEO?
Not necessarily. Many businesses see the most benefit from fixing clarity and consistency issues first, accurate structured data, clear service descriptions, consistent business information, before considering any larger content investment.
What is entity clarity in the context of GEO?
It refers to how confidently a search or AI system can build a single, unambiguous picture of your business from the information available about it. Consistent naming, service descriptions, and structured data across every source you control feed this directly.
Do smaller local businesses actually show up in AI-generated answers?
Yes, when their information is clear and consistent enough for the system to confidently include them. AI citation is not reserved for large or well-known brands, it favors clarity and credibility over sheer size.

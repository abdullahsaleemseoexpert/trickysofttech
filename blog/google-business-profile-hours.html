Google Business Profile Hours: How to Set Regular, Holiday, and Emergency Hours

Wrong hours send urgent customers to your competitors. Here is how to set regular, holiday, and special hours, and how a service business should handle emergency and on-call availability honestly.

Quick answer: To change your Google Business Profile hours, sign in to the Google account that manages the profile, search for your business, choose Edit profile, then Hours. Set your regular weekly schedule first. For a holiday or one-off change, add Special hours for that date, which works for changes of up to six days in a row and only shows if your profile is set to "Open with main hours." Of all the details on a Google Business Profile, opening hours are the one customers act on fastest. Someone with a burst pipe at 9 p.m., a dead furnace on Christmas Eve, or a car stuck on the shoulder does not read your About page. They search, see who looks open, and call. If your profile says you are closed when your phone is on, you lose the job to whoever is shown as open. If it says you are open when nobody answers, you collect a one-star review for a call you never took.

This guide covers how to set regular hours, special and holiday hours, "More hours," and temporary closures. It also covers something most hours guides skip: how a home service or service area business should handle emergency, on-call, and after-hours availability honestly. You will find the holiday dates to confirm for the 2026 to 2027 season, a timeline for getting ready, the structured data that keeps your website in step with your profile, and the problems that cause hours to show wrong. Google changes its menus now and then, so use the labels in your own dashboard as the final word and use this guide to know what to look for.

Why your hours matter more than most owners think

Hours appear in more places than the profile itself. They show in Google Search results, the local pack, the Local Finder, Google Maps, and the knowledge panel that appears when someone searches your business name. They also feed the answers people get when they ask a phone or an AI assistant whether a business is open right now. Every one of those surfaces pulls from the same stored hours, so one wrong entry spreads everywhere.

Three things make hours worth getting exactly right:

  • Searchers filter by "open now." Google Search and Maps both let people narrow results to businesses that are open at that moment. Searches with urgent intent, such as "emergency plumber near me" or "24 hour locksmith," are exactly the searches where this filter gets used. A profile with wrong or missing hours can fall out of those results.
  • Hours are a trust signal. A profile with complete, specific hours looks maintained. A profile with no hours, or hours that contradict your website, looks neglected, and customers treat it that way.
  • Wrong hours create bad reviews. "Showed up and they were closed" and "called three times, no answer" are some of the most common complaints on local profiles. Both come from hours that do not match reality.

Local SEO researchers reported in late 2023 that businesses that were open at the moment of a search seemed to get a lift in visibility, and many businesses responded by switching their hours to 24/7. That was a mistake for most of them. Whatever weight openness carries, the safe reading is that accurate hours help you and false hours eventually hurt you, through reviews, edits by Google or customers, and in the worst case a profile that gets flagged. If you want the wider picture of what moves map pack positions, see how to rank in the Google map pack and local SEO ranking factors in 2026.

The five kinds of hours (and which one you need)

Google gives you several different tools for hours, and using the wrong one is the root of most hours problems. Here is what each one is for.

SettingUse it forKey limits
Main (regular) hoursYour normal weekly scheduleMust be set first. Special hours and More hours depend on it
Special hoursHolidays, events, a day off, early close, a one-time changeTemporary changes or closures of up to six days in a row. One set of special hours cannot exceed 24 hours
More hoursService-specific times, such as delivery, pickup, senior hours, or online serviceNeeds main hours to be set. Does not change whether your profile shows as open or closed
Temporarily closedClosed for seven or more days in a row, or for an unknown length of timeShows a closed label. Turn it off the day you reopen
Permanently closedA business that has shut down for goodDo not use it for a break. It tells Google the business no longer exists

The rule of thumb is simple. If your regular schedule has changed for good, edit main hours. If you will be closed or different for up to six days, use special hours. If you will be gone a week or longer, or you do not know when you are coming back, mark the profile temporarily closed. A holiday weekend is a special hours job, not a temporary closure.

How to set your regular business hours

Your regular hours are the foundation. Get them right before you touch anything else, because special hours only appear when your profile is set to "Open with main hours."

  1. Sign in to the right Google account. Use the account that manages the profile. If you are not sure which account that is, check the guidance in our verification guide first, because you cannot edit a profile you do not manage.
  2. Find your profile. On desktop, search for your business name while signed in and select Edit profile. In the Google Maps app, tap Business, then Edit profile.
  3. Open the Hours section. In the business hours area, choose how the profile should show: open with main hours, temporarily closed, or permanently closed. For a working business, choose open with main hours.
  4. Enter each day. Set the opening and closing time for every day you work, and mark the days you are closed. Use the real times you take calls and start jobs, not the times you would like customers to believe.
  5. Add split hours if you need them. If you close for lunch or run a morning and evening shift, add a second set of hours to the same day instead of stretching one block across the gap.
  6. Save and check your edit status. Google reviews changes before they go live. Use the edit status link in your profile, then search your business in a private browser window to see what the public sees.

Overnight hours and 24-hour days

If you work past midnight, check how your dashboard handles a closing time of, say, 2 a.m. The editor may let you enter it directly for regular hours, while special hours must be split across two entries because a single set cannot run longer than 24 hours. Google's own example splits an overnight shift into one entry ending at 00:00 and another starting at 00:00 the next day.

The hours editor also includes an option for days you are open around the clock. Treat it with care. It is meant for a business that is genuinely staffed every hour of that day. Reserve it for the days when a real person answers and a crew can be dispatched at any time, and read the section on emergency and on-call hours below before you use it.

How to set holiday and special hours

Holiday hours are the most searched hours task and the one most owners forget until the day itself. Google suggests holiday dates in your profile and encourages you to confirm them even when your hours will not change, so that searchers know your listing is accurate.

  1. Confirm your main hours are set and that your profile shows "Open with main hours." If it does not, special hours will not display.
  2. Open Edit profile, then Hours. In the business hours section, find Special hours and choose Edit.
  3. Pick the date. For a date Google has suggested, choose Review. For your own date, choose Add a date and enter it. To change a date you already added, select it.
  4. Set the hours. If you are open, uncheck Closed and enter the times. If you are closed all day, leave Closed checked. For a day with two shifts, use More and then Add hours.
  5. Save. Then check the edit status and the public listing.
  6. Delete the entry after the date passes if you no longer need it. Old special hours clutter the editor, and you do not want a stale one lingering when you reuse the date next year.

The limits to know

  • Up to six days in a row. Special hours cover temporary changes of up to six consecutive days. Longer than that, switch to temporarily closed.
  • No more than 24 hours per entry. An overnight special schedule needs two entries.
  • "Open with main hours" is required. If you marked the profile temporarily closed or left main hours blank, special hours stay hidden.
  • Google does not publish how far ahead you can schedule. The practical answer is to enter holiday hours as soon as you know them, ideally two to three weeks before the date, and to re-check a few days before.

Confirm even when nothing changes

If you are open normal hours on a holiday, still confirm the date. A confirmed holiday tells searchers your hours are accurate. An unconfirmed one can leave a note on your listing warning that holiday hours might differ, which pushes a customer with an emergency to try the next business on the list. For an emergency trade, that note can cost you calls on exactly the days when demand is highest.

Emergency, on-call, and after-hours: what honest hours look like

This is where service businesses get the most wrong, and where most guides say the least. The question is not "what are my hours?" It is "when does a customer who calls actually get a person and a booked job?" Your Google hours should answer that question truthfully.

A good test: if a customer calls at the end of the hours you have posted, does someone answer, and can you commit to sending a technician within a reasonable window? If yes, those are your hours. If the phone goes to voicemail, they are not.

Your situationWhat to set on GoogleWhat to add elsewhere

Business hours, plus an answering service that books jobsYour staffed hours, not 24 hours, unless the service can truly schedule a visit at nightSay "after-hours calls answered, next-morning dispatch" or whatever is true
Business hours, plus an on-call tech for emergenciesRegular hours as main hours. Consider describing the on-call line in the profile descriptionA clear emergency number and a promise you can keep
Appointment onlyThe hours when you take calls and book jobs"By appointment" in your description and on the contact page
Seasonal or storm-driven demandSpecial hours for surge days, with the real timesA website banner and a post when hours change
Single owner-operatorThe hours you can realistically answer the phoneA missed-call text-back so after-hours callers are not lost

Do not fake 24/7

The temptation is obvious: if open-now searches are valuable, switch to 24 hours and capture them all. Here is why that backfires for a business that is not actually staffed overnight:

  • The call goes unanswered. The customer you attracted at 2 a.m. finds voicemail, calls the next company, and may leave a review about it.
  • Reviews expose it. Reviews mentioning no answer after hours are visible to everyone and are hard to remove.
  • It misleads customers. Providing information that does not match reality conflicts with Google's guidelines for representing your business, and that is the sort of detail that can draw edits or enforcement. If your profile is ever suspended, our reinstatement guide explains the process, but it is easier not to need it.
  • It wastes ad and call spend. Calls you cannot take are leads you paid for in effort and got nothing from.

If you want those after-hours searches, fix the operations, not the label. A live answering service with authority to book jobs, a text-back for missed calls, and a clear emergency line cost less than a bad reputation. Our guide to call handling systems for local service businesses covers how to set that up, and the pages for plumbers, HVAC contractors, electricians, towing companies, and locksmiths show how emergency trades turn after-hours demand into booked work.

Hours for service area businesses

If you travel to customers and hide your address, your hours describe when customers can reach you and book work, not when a door is open. There is no storefront for anyone to walk up to, so the question is only whether the phone gets answered and a visit gets scheduled.

  • Post call-and-dispatch hours. Choose the window when you answer and book, even if jobs themselves finish later.
  • Do not post the shop's hours if the shop is your garage. A home address is hidden for a reason, and posting "open to walk-ins" invites visits you do not want.
  • Keep the service area and hours coherent. If you only cover a distant part of your area on weekdays, say so on your website, because Google's hours cannot express that.
  • Check the verification route first. Service area businesses often face video verification, and an inconsistent hours entry is one more thing for a reviewer to question.

Our guide to Google Business Profile for service area businesses covers how to set the address and service area that these hours sit alongside.

Keep hours consistent everywhere

Your Google profile is only one place customers see your hours. If the website footer says 8 to 5, Facebook says 9 to 6, and Google says 7 to 7, you have three versions of the truth, and search systems and AI assistants have to guess which to trust. Consistency is the same principle as name, address, and phone consistency, and the full case is in our guide to NAP consistency and local rankings.

Where to checkWhat to look for
Google Business ProfileMain hours, special hours, and More hours all correct
Website footer and contact pageThe same hours, and a visible note for holiday changes
Website structured dataOpening hours markup that matches the profile
Apple Business Connect and Bing PlacesThe same hours as Google
Facebook, Yelp, and other directoriesMatching hours, updated at the same time
Phone greeting and answering service scriptThe same hours spoken the same way
Online booking form or chat widgetAvailability that matches what you promise

Mark up hours on your website

Structured data lets a machine read your hours without guessing from page text. The openingHoursSpecification property in LocalBusiness markup (or the more specific type for your trade) states your regular hours, and a separate entry with validFrom and validThrough can describe a one-off date. By schema.org convention, a closed day uses an opening and closing time of 00:00. A simplified example:

{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Plumber",
  "name": "Example Plumbing Co.",
  "openingHoursSpecification": [
    {
      "@type": "OpeningHoursSpecification",
      "dayOfWeek": ["Monday","Tuesday","Wednesday","Thursday","Friday"],
      "opens": "07:00",
      "closes": "18:00"
    },
    {
      "@type": "OpeningHoursSpecification",
      "opens": "00:00",
      "closes": "00:00",
      "validFrom": "2026-11-26",
      "validThrough": "2026-11-26"
    }
  ]
}

Test any markup with a validator before you publish it, and remove the one-off entry after the date passes. For the wider picture of what to mark up and why, see our guides to LocalBusiness schema and structured data for AI search.

Do hours affect your ranking?

The honest answer has two parts. First, accurate hours are required to appear in open-now searches, and those are some of the most valuable searches a service business can win. If you are marked closed during your real working hours, you are invisible to anyone who filters by open now. Second, there is no reliable evidence that stretching your hours beyond reality improves your position for long, and plenty of reason to think the downside outweighs the upside.

The healthy approach is to treat hours as a hygiene factor. Get them right, keep them right, and put the effort that moves the needle elsewhere: your primary category, your service list, photos, and above all reviews and prominence. Hours do not substitute for any of those. Our Google Business Profile optimization checklist shows where hours fit in the rest of the profile, and how to get Google reviews covers the signal that usually matters most.

One more practical point: openness is checked against your hours at the time of the search. If you work until 7 p.m. but your profile says 5 p.m., every search between 5 and 7 is a search you cannot win through the open-now filter. Accurate hours are not just defensive. They can add visible searches you are already able to serve.

Temporarily closed, permanently closed, and the pitfalls

Temporarily closed is the right setting when you will be closed for seven or more days in a row, or when you do not know how long it will last. Think of a storm that floods your shop, a long vacation, a medical leave, or a truck out of service for repairs with no schedule.

It comes with trade-offs you should understand before you use it:

  • It shows a closed label. Customers see it immediately, and searchers using open-now filters will not see you.
  • It may be shown less prominently. A profile that is closed is a worse answer to most searches, so do not expect it to hold the same position.
  • It is easy to forget. A profile left on temporarily closed after you reopen is silent lost revenue. Put a reminder on your calendar for the day you plan to return.
  • It is not for holidays. A weekend closure belongs in special hours. Using temporarily closed for a normal holiday takes you out of searches for longer than necessary.

Permanently closed is a different thing entirely. It tells Google the business has ceased to exist. Marking it by mistake, or having someone else suggest it, can take you out of results, and reversing it can require a support request. If your profile shows permanently closed and you are still operating, treat it like any other profile problem and start with the steps in our guide on why your Google Business Profile is not showing up.

If hours keep changing back after you fix them, treat it as a warning, not a one-off. Look for pending suggested edits, check who has access to the profile under its managers list, and remove any third-party tool you no longer use. A competitor changing your hours is rare but not unheard of, which is one more reason to review your profile regularly. Managing hours across many locations

If you run more than a handful of locations, setting hours profile by profile is slow and error-prone. Google supports setting special hours through a bulk upload spreadsheet. In that file, you enter the date in year-month-day format (for example, 2026-12-25) along with the hours, or an "x" to show a full-day closure, then import the file.

One warning from Google's own guidance is worth repeating: when you are only adding special hours to locations that already exist, include only the store code and the special hours columns in your file. Empty columns can erase data already on the profiles. Test with one or two locations first, then roll out the rest.

Multi-location owners should also keep a single master schedule that every location's hours and holiday plan comes from, so you are never updating twenty profiles from memory. The wider strategy for managing profiles at scale is in our guide to multi-location local SEO.

A holiday-readiness timeline

Holiday hours go wrong when they are handled on the day. Work backward from the date, and you will never be caught out.

WhenWhat to do
Three weeks beforeDecide your holiday schedule: closed, reduced, or emergency only. Confirm who is on call and who answers the phone
Two weeks beforeEnter special hours on your Google profile. Update your website, Facebook, Apple and Bing listings. Add a short note to your phone greeting
One week beforePost an update on your profile and social accounts. Check that the special hours show on the public listing
Day beforeRe-check the listing, the website, and the phone greeting. Test the emergency line

Posting an update is worth doing for reasons beyond hours. A short post about holiday availability keeps the profile active and gives customers an answer before they ask. The guidance on what to post and how often is in our profile optimization checklist.

Ten hours mistakes that cost calls

  1. Forgetting holiday hours until the day. By then, customers have already seen the "hours might differ" note and moved on.
  2. Setting 24/7 hours without 24/7 staffing. You attract calls you cannot take and earn reviews you do not want.
  3. Leaving the profile on temporarily closed after reopening. This quietly removes you from open-now results.
  4. Using temporarily closed for a short holiday. Special hours are built for that, and they keep you visible.
  5. Changing hours on Google but not on the website. Mismatched hours confuse customers and AI systems.
  6. Entering overnight hours as one block in special hours. A single entry cannot exceed 24 hours, so overnight needs two.
  7. Not setting main hours first. Without "Open with main hours," special hours never display.
  8. Posting the shop's hours for a hidden-address business. Your hours should describe when you take calls and book jobs.
  9. Never checking the public listing. What you see in the dashboard is not always what customers see.
  10. Letting a tool or team member overwrite hours without noticing. Review who has access and what is connected.

Hours and AI answers

More and more "is this business open?" questions are answered by an assistant instead of a list of links. Whether it is a phone assistant, a map app, or an AI search tool, the answer comes from structured facts the system trusts, and your hours are among the easiest facts to get wrong. When your Google profile, website markup, and directory listings all agree, a system can state your hours confidently. When they conflict, it may hedge or choose another business.

That is why consistent hours are both a local SEO task and an AI visibility task. Your hours checklist

  • Main hours are set for every day, with split shifts and closed days entered correctly.
  • The profile shows "Open with main hours," so special hours can display.
  • Holiday dates are confirmed for the season, including days you are open as normal.
  • Hours match reality, including on-call and after-hours handling that you can actually deliver.
  • The website footer and contact page show the same hours and any holiday notice.
  • Opening hours markup on your site matches your profile and has been tested.
  • Other listings (Apple, Bing, Facebook, directories) carry the same hours.
  • Temporary closures are off on the day you reopen, with a calendar reminder set.
  • Access is reviewed: only the right people and tools can edit the profile.
  • The public listing was checked in a private window after every change.

Common Questions

Frequently asked.

How do I change my business hours on Google?
Sign in to the Google account that manages your profile, search for your business, choose Edit profile, then Hours. Update your regular weekly schedule, save, and check the edit status. Google reviews changes before they go live, so confirm the result on the public listing in a private browser window.
How do I set holiday hours on Google Business Profile?
Open Edit profile, then Hours, find Special hours, and choose Edit. Review a date Google suggests or add your own, then mark the day closed or enter the hours you will be open. Save, then check the public listing. Confirm official holidays even when your hours do not change.
Why are my special hours not showing on Google?
The most common cause is that the profile is not set to Open with main hours, which special hours require. Other causes are an edit still under review, an overnight entry longer than 24 hours, or a change that has not reached Maps or Search yet. Check the edit status and set main hours first.
How far in advance can I set holiday hours?
Google does not publish a specific limit. The practical approach is to enter holiday hours as soon as you know them, ideally two to three weeks before the date, and to re-check the public listing a few days before.
Should I use temporarily closed or special hours for a short break?
Use special hours for closures of up to six days in a row, such as a holiday weekend. Use temporarily closed when you will be closed for seven days or more, or for an unknown length of time, and turn it off the day you reopen.
Can I set 24-hour hours if I only take emergency calls?
Only if a real person answers and a crew can be dispatched at any hour. If calls go to voicemail overnight, posting 24-hour hours attracts calls you cannot take and invites bad reviews. Post the hours you can truly deliver and describe your emergency process on your website.
How long does it take for hours changes to appear?
Google reviews edits before they go live, and Google does not publish a set time. Some changes appear quickly and others take longer. Use the edit status link in your profile, and search your business in a private window to see what customers see.
What hours should a service area business show?
Show the hours when customers can reach you, get an answer, and book a visit. Because there is no storefront, do not post shop hours or walk-in times for a home address you hide. Describe after-hours handling on your website and in your profile description.
Do business hours affect Google Maps ranking?
Accurate hours let you appear in open-now searches, which matter for urgent services. There is no reliable evidence that stretching your hours beyond reality improves rankings for long, and false hours bring bad reviews. Keep hours accurate and focus ranking effort on category, reviews, and prominence.
